Morphosis

Founded 1972 · American, based in Los Angeles · 7 buildings on the wall

Morphosis is an architecture and design practice based in Los Angeles and New York City, founded informally in 1972 by Michael Brickler, Thom Mayne, Livio Santini and James Stafford; Michael Rotondi was a principal from 1975 to 1991. Thom Mayne, its leading figure, was a founding member of the Southern California Institute of Architecture (SCI-Arc) and won the Pritzker Prize in 2005.

Read moreShow less

The Graduate House, University of Toronto (2000) and Diamond Ranch High School in Pomona (2000) come from its rise in the 1990s. The San Francisco Federal Building (2007) uses daylight for 80 percent of its space, and its south wall of perforated stainless-steel panels warms in the sun to pull cool air through sensor-controlled windows; it was the first federal building certified under LEED. 41 Cooper Square in New York (2009) wraps a full-height atrium in an operable skin of perforated stainless steel. The Perot Museum of Nature and Science in Dallas (2012) is a large cube floating over a landscaped base, with an escalator in a glass tube slanting outside it. Morphosis's projects are studied for layered building skins and climate-driven design.
