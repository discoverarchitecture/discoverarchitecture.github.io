Modernist architecture

934 buildings

Modernist architecture builds with new construction technology and rejects ornament, so volume, structure and plan carry the whole design. It emerged at the end of the 19th century and was dominant from the Second World War until the 1980s. In 1932 Hitchcock and Johnson's exhibition at MoMA set out its principles: architecture as volume, regularity in the facade rather than symmetry, and no applied ornament. Under the name International Style it spread throughout the world. Le Corbusier's white houses of the 1920s had no ornament inside or out.

Read moreShow less

The Fagus Factory in Alfeld (1911), by Walter Gropius and Adolf Meyer, sets its glass curtain wall forward of the piers, so the corners turn with no column. Gropius's Bauhaus Dessau (1926) wraps its workshops in glass on a pinwheel plan. Mies van der Rohe's Villa Tugendhat in Brno (1930) brings the open plan into a family home, with glass walls that drop away to the garden. Le Corbusier's Villa Savoye in Poissy (1931) puts his Five Points of Architecture into a single house. Mies's Seagram Building in New York (1958) stands back behind a plaza, its bronze mullions expressing the frame.
