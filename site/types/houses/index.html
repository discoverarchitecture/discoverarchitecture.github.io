Famous houses by architects

321 buildings

Famous houses by architects are small buildings that carry big arguments. A house can express the opinions of the people who build it or live in it, and its form is shaped by the materials at hand, so a single house can set out a whole way of building. In January 1945 John Entenza's magazine Arts & Architecture announced the Case Study Houses, to produce inexpensive modern model homes that could be built again and again.

Read moreShow less

Frank Lloyd Wright completed Fallingwater for the Kaufmann family in 1937, with a guest wing in 1939; its concrete terraces cantilever over a waterfall. Le Corbusier built the Villa Savoye in Poissy between 1928 and 1931 in reinforced concrete, putting his Five Points of Architecture into one house; it had an enormous influence on international modernism. Mies van der Rohe's Farnsworth House (1951) is a single glass room lifted above a floodplain on eight steel columns. Charles and Ray Eames built the Eames House in Los Angeles for themselves in 1949 from mass-produced parts. Robert Venturi's Vanna Venturi House in Philadelphia (1964) is one of the first prominent works of postmodern architecture.
