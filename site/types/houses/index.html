Famous houses by architects

Famous houses by architects are small buildings that carry big arguments. A house can express the opinions of the people who build it or live in it, and its form is shaped by the materials at hand, so a single house can set out a whole way of building. In January 1945 John Entenza's magazine Arts & Architecture announced the Case Study Houses, to produce inexpensive modern model homes that could be built again and again.

Frank Lloyd Wright completed Fallingwater for the Kaufmann family in 1937, with a guest wing in 1939; its concrete terraces cantilever over a waterfall. Le Corbusier built the Villa Savoye in Poissy between 1928 and 1931 in reinforced concrete, putting his Five Points of Architecture into one house; it had an enormous influence on international modernism. Mies van der Rohe's Farnsworth House (1951) is a single glass room lifted above a floodplain on eight steel columns. Charles and Ray Eames built the Eames House in Los Angeles for themselves in 1949 from mass-produced parts. Robert Venturi's Vanna Venturi House in Philadelphia (1964) is one of the first prominent works of postmodern architecture.

Questions students ask

Which famous houses should architecture students study?

A core list: Frank Lloyd Wright's Robie House in Chicago (1910) and Fallingwater (completed 1937); the Rietveld Schröder House in Utrecht (1924); Le Corbusier's Villa Savoye in Poissy, built 1928 to 1931; Mies van der Rohe's Villa Tugendhat (1930) and Farnsworth House (1951); Alvar Aalto's Villa Mairea (1939); the Eames House (1949); and Robert Venturi's Vanna Venturi House (1964).

Why are houses important in architecture?

Houses matter because they are small buildings that make large arguments. A house can voice the views of those who build or inhabit it, and the materials close to hand shape its form, so one house can lay out an entire way of building. Villa Savoye, made of reinforced concrete, gathers Le Corbusier's Five Points of Architecture in a single house and had a huge influence on modernism.

What were the Case Study Houses?

The Case Study Houses were a programme, announced in January 1945 in Arts & Architecture, John Entenza's magazine, to produce low-cost, modern model homes able to be built many times over. The best known is the Eames House (1949) in Los Angeles, which Charles and Ray Eames built for themselves out of mass-produced parts.

What are the most famous modernist glass houses?

The best known include the Farnsworth House in Plano (1951) by Mies van der Rohe, one glass room raised on eight steel columns over a floodplain, a steel-and-glass box in the International Style. Philip Johnson's Glass House in New Canaan (1949) is a glass box with the landscape for walls; only the bathroom is closed off. Earlier, Mies's Villa Tugendhat in Brno (1930) has glass walls that drop away to the garden.
