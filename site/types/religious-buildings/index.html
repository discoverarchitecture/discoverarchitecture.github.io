Religious architecture: churches, mosques and temples

455 buildings

Religious architecture designs places of worship, whose plan and form carry belief. Ancient temples were often seen as a god's dwelling. A Hindu temple is read as a model of the universe and of inner space, its sanctum crowned by a tower, the shikhara, called the vimana in the south. The earliest mosques were hypostyle halls with courtyards for Friday prayer, and the Ottomans introduced the central-dome mosque in the 15th century. Gothic architecture began in 12th-century France at the abbey church of Saint-Denis, and its defining feature is the pointed arch. In the 20th century, steel and concrete changed church design.

Hagia Sophia in Istanbul (537) was one of the first buildings with a fully pendentive dome. The Sultan Ahmed Mosque (Blue Mosque), from 1617, rings its central dome with four semi-domes. The Great Mosque of Djenné in Mali (1907) is built of mud brick, its palm-wood beams left as fixed scaffolding for re-plastering. Le Corbusier's Notre-Dame du Haut in Ronchamp (1955) punches thick walls with deep, coloured windows that model the light. Tadao Ando's Church of the Light in Ibaraki (1989) cuts a cross into a concrete wall.
