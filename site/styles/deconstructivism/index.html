Deconstructivist architecture

75 buildings

Deconstructivist architecture breaks a building into fragments, with no obvious harmony, continuity or symmetry; its look is often described as controlled chaos. The name plays on Russian Constructivism and on the ideas of the French philosopher Jacques Derrida. The style came to public notice with the 1982 competition for the Parc de la Villette. In 1988 Mark Wigley and Philip Johnson curated an exhibition at MoMA that crystallised the movement and made its seven architects famous: Peter Eisenman, Frank Gehry, Zaha Hadid, Coop Himmelblau, Rem Koolhaas, Daniel Libeskind and Bernard Tschumi. Gehry refused the label, as he refused any other.

Eisenman's Wexner Center for the Arts in Columbus (1989) runs a white steel scaffold grid along two shifted city grids, splitting fragments of a brick armoury. Gehry's Vitra Design Museum in Weil am Rhein (1989) collides white plastered volumes around skylit galleries. In the same town, the Vitra Fire Station (1993), Hadid's first built work, is a set of sharp concrete planes caught in motion. Libeskind's Imperial War Museum North in Manchester (2002) is three fragmented, intersecting curved volumes, shards of earth, air and water from a shattered globe.

Questions students ask

What is deconstructivist architecture?

Deconstructivism splits a building into pieces that lack evident harmony, continuity or symmetry, a result many describe as controlled chaos. Its name is a play on Russian Constructivism and draws on the thinking of the French philosopher Jacques Derrida. It first caught public attention through the 1982 Parc de la Villette competition in Paris, and an exhibition at MoMA in 1988 gave the movement its definite shape.

Who are the main deconstructivist architects?

The 1988 MoMA exhibition curated by Mark Wigley and Philip Johnson brought fame to seven: Zaha Hadid, Rem Koolhaas, Frank Gehry, Daniel Libeskind, Peter Eisenman, Bernard Tschumi and Coop Himmelblau. The label sits uneasily on some of them. Gehry turned it down, as he turned down every label, even though his Guggenheim Bilbao (1997) and Walt Disney Concert Hall (2003) often appear as examples of it.

Which deconstructivist buildings should architecture students study?

Set three side by side: the Wexner Center for the Arts (1989) in Columbus by Eisenman, the Vitra Design Museum (1989) by Gehry and Hadid's first completed building, the Vitra Fire Station (1993); the last two stand in Weil am Rhein. Next look at Libeskind's Jewish Museum Berlin (2001), a zigzag plan crossed by voids that can be seen but not entered, and his Imperial War Museum North in Manchester (2002), made of broken, intersecting curved volumes.

Where can I see deconstructivist buildings?

Germany has a cluster: the Vitra Design Museum and Vitra Fire Station in Weil am Rhein, BMW Welt in Munich, and the Jewish Museum Berlin. Frank Gehry's work is spread widely, from the Guggenheim Bilbao and the Dancing House in Prague to the Walt Disney Concert Hall in Los Angeles and the Biomuseo in Panama City. In Rotterdam, OMA's Kunsthal threads ramps through a box.
