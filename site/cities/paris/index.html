Architecture in Paris

59 buildings

Paris is a city where styles were born and then set into a single, ordered plan. Gothic architecture began in the rebuilding of the choir of Saint-Denis, finished in 1144, and around 1160 Maurice de Sully used it at a far larger scale for Notre-Dame. The city imposed formal height limits on its buildings in 1667. In 1853 Napoleon III made Haussmann prefect of the Seine, starting the rebuilding of Paris; the programme ended when Haussmann was dismissed in January 1870. The world's fairs of 1889 and 1900 added the Eiffel Tower and the Grand Palais, and the design for the Centre Pompidou was chosen from 681 competition entries.

Charles Garnier's Palais Garnier (1875) turns its grand staircase and foyers into a promenade as theatrical as the stage. Gustave Eiffel's Eiffel Tower (1889) gathers four curving wrought-iron legs into a single tapering shaft. Charles Girault's Grand Palais (1900) hides a riveted steel-and-glass vaulted nave behind stone Beaux-Arts facades. Renzo Piano and Richard Rogers's Centre Pompidou (1977) moves its structure and colour-coded services outside, leaving free floors and a public piazza.
