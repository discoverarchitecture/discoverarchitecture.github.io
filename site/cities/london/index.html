Architecture in London

115 buildings

London's architecture is worth studying because the city has been rebuilt again and again on its old ground. The Great Fire of 1666, which destroyed about nine tenths of the city, was a turning point. Wren's grid plan was set aside: the city was rebuilt on its medieval streets, under a 1667 act asking for brick houses of uniform height and plot size. Pevsner praised Wren's dome for St Paul's as among the most perfect in the world. Most of the city walls came down in the 18th century so London could grow; Victorian London built the world's first underground railway. John Nash laid out Regent Street with James Burton and remodelled Buckingham House into Buckingham Palace.

The Palace of Westminster (1876), by Charles Barry and Augustus Pugin, wraps the parliament in Perpendicular Gothic Revival detail. Battersea Power Station (1955) is one of the world's largest brick buildings. The Barbican Estate (1982) lifts housing, arts and a lake onto walkways above the street. The Lloyd's Building (1986) sets its lifts, stairs and ducts in plug-in towers around a 60-metre atrium. The Shard (2012) became London's first supertall skyscraper.

Questions students ask

Why is London's architecture worth studying?

London is worth studying because it has been rebuilt many times over on the same ground. The turning point was the Great Fire of 1666, which wiped out roughly nine tenths of the city. Wren's grid plan was dropped, and rebuilding followed the medieval street pattern, under an act of 1667 that called for brick houses of a uniform height and plot size. Later, Victorian London built an underground railway before any other city in the world.

Which modern buildings in London should architecture students visit?

A good route takes in Denys Lasdun's Royal National Theatre (1976), whose concrete terraces step toward the Thames; the Barbican Estate (1982); Richard Rogers's Lloyd's Building (1986); Tate Modern (2000), a power station turned museum; Foster and Partners' 30 St Mary Axe (2004), a diagrid tower; and Renzo Piano's The Shard (2012), London's first supertall skyscraper.

Where can I see brutalist architecture in London?

London has some of the best-known brutalist buildings anywhere. The Barbican Estate and Barbican Centre (1982) lift housing, an arts centre and a lake onto walkways above the street. Ernő Goldfinger's Balfron Tower (1967) and Trellick Tower (1972) put their lifts in separate towers linked to the flats by bridges every third floor, and the Royal National Theatre (1976) sits on the South Bank.

Which historic buildings in London should architecture students know?

St Paul's Cathedral comes first: Pevsner ranked Wren's dome there among the world's most perfect. John Nash planned Regent Street together with James Burton and turned Buckingham House into Buckingham Palace. Sir John Soane's Museum (1824) draws daylight down through narrow, layered rooms, and Charles Barry and Augustus Pugin's Palace of Westminster (1876) covers the parliament building with Perpendicular Gothic Revival detail.
