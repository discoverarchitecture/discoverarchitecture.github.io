Architecture in Tokyo

73 buildings

Tokyo's buildings share no single style, but the city is a key place to study modern Japanese architecture: it was left in ruins twice in recent history and rebuilt each time. The Great Kanto earthquake of 1923 began an era of concrete building, and an air raid in March 1945 burned much of the city. The 1964 Olympics marked its rebirth. Metabolism was launched with a manifesto for the 1960 World Design Conference in Tokyo, treating the city as a living organism. Kenzo Tange mentored the Metabolists without joining them.

Tange's Yoyogi National Gymnasium (1964), built for the Olympics, hangs a suspension roof from two concrete masts; his Pritzker citation counted it "among the most beautiful buildings of the 20th century". His St. Mary's Cathedral (1964) joins eight concrete shells in a cross-shaped skylight. The Kasumigaseki Building (1968) was Japan's first modern skyscraper, 36 floors on a flexible steel frame built to ride out earthquakes. Kisho Kurokawa's Nakagin Capsule Tower (1972) bolts prefabricated capsules to two cores, the world's first example of capsule architecture. Nikken Sekkei's Tokyo Skytree (2012) damps sway with a concrete core, like a pagoda's central column.

Questions students ask

Why is Tokyo important for modern architecture?

Tokyo is a key city for studying modern Japanese architecture, even though its buildings follow no one style. Twice in recent history it was reduced to ruins and rebuilt: the 1923 Great Kanto earthquake opened an era of concrete construction, and a March 1945 air raid burned large parts of the city. The 1964 Olympics signalled its rebirth as a modern city.

What is Metabolism in architecture?

Metabolism is an architectural movement launched through a manifesto prepared for Tokyo's 1960 World Design Conference, which saw the city as a living organism that keeps renewing itself. Kenzo Tange acted as a mentor to the Metabolists but did not join them. The Nakagin Capsule Tower (1972) by Kisho Kurokawa attaches prefabricated capsules to two cores, applying the Metabolist idea of replaceable parts, and it pioneered capsule architecture.

Which buildings in Tokyo should architecture students visit?

Begin with Le Corbusier's National Museum of Western Art (1959) and Kenzo Tange's Yoyogi National Gymnasium (1964), whose suspension roof hangs from two concrete masts and which his Pritzker citation praised. Add Tange's St. Mary's Cathedral (1964), Fumihiko Maki's Spiral (1985), Rafael Viñoly's Tokyo International Forum (1996), Renzo Piano's Maison Hermès (2001) and Kengo Kuma's Japan National Stadium (2019).

How do Tokyo's buildings deal with earthquakes?

After the 1923 Great Kanto earthquake the city moved toward concrete construction. On the wall, Renzo Piano's Maison Hermès (2001) sets its glass-block skin on a frame designed to flex in earthquakes, and Nikken Sekkei's Tokyo Skytree (2012) uses a concrete core as a damping column, like the central column of a pagoda, as its triangular base turns into a round plan.
