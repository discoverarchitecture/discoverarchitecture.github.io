Religious architecture: churches, mosques and temples

456 buildings

Religious architecture designs places of worship, whose plan and form carry belief. Ancient temples were often seen as a god's dwelling. A Hindu temple is read as a model of the universe and of inner space, its sanctum crowned by a tower, the shikhara, called the vimana in the south. The earliest mosques were hypostyle halls with courtyards for Friday prayer, and the Ottomans introduced the central-dome mosque in the 15th century. Gothic architecture began in 12th-century France at the abbey church of Saint-Denis, and its defining feature is the pointed arch. In the 20th century, steel and concrete changed church design.

Hagia Sophia in Istanbul (537) was one of the first buildings with a fully pendentive dome. The Sultan Ahmed Mosque (Blue Mosque), from 1617, rings its central dome with four semi-domes. The Great Mosque of Djenné in Mali (1907) is built of mud brick, its palm-wood beams left as fixed scaffolding for re-plastering. Le Corbusier's Notre-Dame du Haut in Ronchamp (1955) punches thick walls with deep, coloured windows that model the light. Tadao Ando's Church of the Light in Ibaraki (1989) cuts a cross into a concrete wall.

Questions students ask

What is sacred architecture?

Sacred architecture means designing and constructing places of worship, where a building's plan and form express belief. Temples in the ancient world were often thought of as the home of a god. A Hindu temple is understood as a model of inner space as well as of the universe, with a tower over its sanctum known as the shikhara, or the vimana in the south. Bahá'í houses of worship have nine sides and nine entrances.

How did mosque architecture develop?

Mosques began as hypostyle halls, first developed under the Umayyads, with courtyards to hold the Friday prayer crowds. In the 15th century the Ottomans brought in the central-dome mosque: in Istanbul, Mimar Sinan's Süleymaniye Mosque (1557) braces its dome with half-domes, and in Edirne the Selimiye Mosque (1575) rests a single wide dome on eight piers. Modern mosques reinterpret both types, as Marina Tabassum's Bait Ur Rouf Mosque in Dhaka (2012) does.

Which religious buildings should architecture students study?

Start with Hagia Sophia (537) in Istanbul, among the earliest buildings to have a fully pendentive dome. Mali's Great Mosque of Djenné (1907) is made of mud brick, with palm-wood beams kept in place as permanent scaffolding. Then compare Notre-Dame du Haut (1955) in Ronchamp by Le Corbusier, Oscar Niemeyer's Cathedral of Brasília (1970), the Church of the Light (1989) by Tadao Ando and the Lotus Temple in New Delhi (1986).

How did modern materials change church design?

Steel and concrete changed the way churches were designed in the 20th century. In Tokyo, Kenzo Tange's St. Mary's Cathedral (1964) brings eight hyperbolic paraboloid shells together in a cross-shaped skylight; Niemeyer's Cathedral of Brasília surrounds a sunken nave with sixteen curved concrete columns; and Ando cuts a cross of light into a concrete wall. Gothic, by contrast, which began with Saint-Denis's abbey church in 12th-century France, was defined by the pointed arch.
