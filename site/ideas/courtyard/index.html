Courtyard in architecture

325 buildings

A courtyard is an area open to the sky, often surrounded by a building, bringing light, air and privacy into the middle of a plan, which people want nearly everywhere. In warm weather an open central court helps cool a house. Courtyard houses go back to the Neolithic, around 6400–6000 BC, and courtyards were widely used in the ancient Middle East. The open centre of a Roman house was called the atrium, and in Morocco's old towns the courtyard house has always been the standard form.

In the Alhambra in Granada the basic unit of the palace is a rectangular courtyard, each room opening to the sky. The Shah Mosque, Isfahan (1629) rings its central courtyard with four monumental iwans. Louis Kahn's Salk Institute in La Jolla (1965) sets its laboratories around a travertine plaza open to the Pacific. Tadao Ando's Row House in Sumiyoshi in Osaka (1976) splits a concrete box with an open courtyard you cross to get to bed, even in the rain. Foster + Partners' Kogod Courtyard, National Portrait Gallery in Washington, D.C. (2007) covers a 19th-century courtyard with an undulating glass canopy.

Questions students ask

What is a courtyard in architecture?

A courtyard is a space open to the sky, often enclosed by a building, that brings light, air and privacy into the heart of a plan, qualities wanted almost everywhere. When the weather is warm, an open central court helps keep a house cool. Courtyard houses date from the Neolithic, roughly 6400–6000 BC, and in a Roman house the open central space was known as the atrium.

What are good examples of courtyard architecture?

Good examples start with the Alhambra in Granada, where the palace is built up from rectangular courtyards as its basic unit. In Isfahan, the Shah Mosque (1629) sets four monumental iwans around its central courtyard. Louis Kahn's Salk Institute (1965) arranges its labs around a travertine plaza that opens to the Pacific, and Alvar Aalto's Säynätsalo Town Hall (1952) groups brick rooms around a raised courtyard of grass.

Which courtyard houses should architecture students study?

For modern courtyard houses, compare Luis Barragán's house in Mexico City (1948), Alvar Aalto's Villa Mairea (1939), with its L-shaped courtyard, and the Row House in Sumiyoshi (1976) by Tadao Ando, whose interior courtyard admits light and must be crossed to move between rooms. The type is old: the ancient Middle East used courtyards widely, and in the old towns of Morocco, houses have always been built around courtyards as the norm.

How do contemporary buildings use courtyards?

Contemporary buildings still use the courtyard to organise large plans. In I. M. Pei's Suzhou Museum (2006), white walls enclose a water garden. The Interlace in Singapore (2013), by OMA and Ole Scheeren, stacks thirty-one blocks in a hexagonal weave that creates large garden courtyards. In Washington, D.C., the Kogod Courtyard (2007) at the National Portrait Gallery, by Foster + Partners, roofs a 19th-century courtyard with a rippling glass canopy.
