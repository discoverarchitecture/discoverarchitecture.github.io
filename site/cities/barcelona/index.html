Architecture in Barcelona

47 buildings

Barcelona's architecture is worth studying for one plan and one movement. Ildefons Cerdà's Eixample of 1859, the city's biggest expansion, laid a strict grid of wide avenues and square blocks with chamfered corners. Modernisme ran from the city's first World Fair in 1888 to 1911; Antoni Gaudí is its best-known architect, and Lluís Domènech i Montaner created a genuine alternative to his work. For the 1929 Exposition, Mies van der Rohe built the German Pavilion, demolished in 1930 and rebuilt permanently in 1983–86.

Gaudí's Casa Batlló (1906) wraps a bone-like facade around a light well tiled from deep to pale blue to even out the daylight. His Casa Milà (1912) carries an undulating stone facade on columns, with two light courts and a catenary-arched attic. The Sagrada Familia, begun in 1882, filters coloured daylight into its nave through branching, tree-like columns and hyperboloid vaults. Domènech i Montaner's Palau de la Música Catalana (1908) uses a steel frame to open walls of glass and a stained-glass skylight over the concert hall. Mies van der Rohe and Lilly Reich's Barcelona Pavilion (1929) sets free-standing walls under a floating roof.

Questions students ask

Why is Barcelona important in architecture?

Barcelona matters chiefly for a single plan and a single movement. Ildefons Cerdà's 1859 Eixample, the largest expansion the city has seen, set out a grid of broad avenues and blocks with cut-off corners. Modernisme lasted from Barcelona's first World Fair, held in 1888, until 1911. Earlier layers remain as well: the city started as the Roman town of Barcino, and its oldest parts are in the Gothic Quarter.

Which Gaudí buildings should architecture students visit?

Start with Casa Vicens (1885), Antoni Gaudí's first major project; Palau Güell (1890), whose central hall sits beneath a perforated dome; Casa Batlló (1906), where tiles in the light well shade from deep to pale blue so the daylight is even throughout; Casa Milà (1912) and its undulating stone facade; Park Güell (1914); and the Sagrada Familia, started in 1882. Gaudí is the most famous Modernisme architect.

Who was Domènech i Montaner?

Lluís Domènech i Montaner was a Modernisme architect whose work gave a real alternative to Gaudí's. His Palau de la Música Catalana (1908) relies on a steel frame to open up glass walls and a stained-glass skylight above the concert hall. His Hospital de Sant Pau places brick ward pavilions among gardens, joined by underground service tunnels, so the hospital reads as a garden of small buildings.

Which modern buildings in Barcelona should architecture students visit?

Start with the Barcelona Pavilion by Mies van der Rohe and Lilly Reich: built for the 1929 Exposition, it was pulled down in 1930 and permanently reconstructed in 1983–86. Then see Josep Lluís Sert's Fundació Joan Miró (1975), Arata Isozaki's Palau Sant Jordi (1990), Jean Nouvel's Torre Glòries (2005), Enric Ruiz-Geli's Media-TIC (2010), wrapped in ETFE cushions, and Lacol's La Borda (2018), cooperative housing in cross-laminated timber.
