Art Deco architecture

97 buildings

Art Deco architecture pairs bold geometry with rich decoration: setbacks, sunbursts, metal ornament and decorated crowns. It first appeared in Paris in the 1910s, just before the First World War, growing from the geometric forms of the Vienna Secession and Cubism. Its name comes from the 1925 exhibition of decorative and industrial arts in Paris. Reinforced concrete gave more freedom of form. It was an international style that flourished in the 1920s and early 1930s, and Miami Beach holds the largest concentration of Art Deco buildings in the world.

Read moreShow less

Josef Hoffmann's Stoclet Palace in Brussels (1911) is described as a prototype of the style: white marble planes edged with bronze mouldings make its walls read as thin panels. Auguste Perret's Théâtre des Champs-Élysées in Paris (1913) spans its auditorium with a concrete frame behind a flat marble front. In New York, the Chrysler Building (1930) stacks a stainless-steel crown of sunburst arches above its setbacks, and the Empire State Building (1931) was the world's tallest building until 1970. The Palais de Chaillot in Paris (1937) curves two wings around a terrace that opens a view to the Eiffel Tower.

Questions students ask

What is Art Deco architecture?

Art Deco architecture combines strong geometric forms with lavish ornament such as setbacks, sunbursts, metalwork and decorated crowns. It began in Paris during the 1910s, shortly before the First World War, building on the geometry of the Vienna Secession and Cubism. The name was taken from the 1925 exhibition in Paris devoted to decorative and industrial arts. An international style, it was at its height through the 1920s and the early 1930s.

What are the key features of Art Deco buildings?

Look for strong geometry combined with lavish decoration: decorated crowns, sunbursts, metal ornament and stepped setbacks. Reinforced concrete let architects shape forms with more freedom. New York's Chrysler Building (1930) crowns itself with sunburst arches of stainless steel above its setbacks; Radio City Music Hall (1932) forms its auditorium ceiling from nested semicircles; and Chicago's Adler Planetarium (1930) is a twelve-sided block of rainbow granite.

Which Art Deco buildings should architecture students study?

New York's Chrysler (1930) and Empire State (1931) buildings are the style's best-known skyscraper monuments. Earlier, the Théâtre des Champs-Élysées (1913) in Paris, by Auguste Perret, spanned the auditorium with a concrete frame set behind a flat marble facade. Then compare Detroit's Fisher Building (1928) and its barrel-vaulted arcade with the stepped Kavanagh building (1936) in Buenos Aires.

Where can I see Art Deco architecture?

Miami Beach has more Art Deco buildings concentrated together than anywhere else in the world, and New York has its skyscrapers, from 30 Rockefeller Plaza to 70 Pine Street. Because the style was international, examples reach far beyond: the Parliament House in Helsinki, the Manila Metropolitan Theater with its Philippine plant motifs, the Australian War Memorial in Canberra and the Estadio Centenario in Montevideo, built for the first World Cup.
