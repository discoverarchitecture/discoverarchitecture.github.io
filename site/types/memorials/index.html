Memorial architecture

111 buildings

Memorial architecture gives remembering a place: a memorial is an object or place that focuses memory or commemoration. The most common kind is a gravestone or a plaque, and temporary, makeshift memorials are called grassroots memorials. A mausoleum is a monument that encloses a burial chamber; the word comes from the tomb of Mausolus at Halicarnassus. Maya Lin's Vietnam Veterans Memorial, dedicated in 1982, is two black granite walls, and its colour, lack of ornament and form below ground were controversial at first.

Peter Eisenman's Memorial to the Murdered Jews of Europe in Berlin (2005) sets 2,711 concrete stelae on a sloping grid, forming narrow aisles; critics said its lack of names or information weakens its message. Karl Friedrich Schinkel's Neue Wache in Berlin (1818), a Neoclassical guardhouse, is now a memorial lit by an oculus. Anitkabir in Ankara (1953), the mausoleum of Atatürk, leads a ceremonial road lined with lions to a vast court. Mazar-e-Quaid in Karachi (1970) raises a white marble cube with pointed arches on a platform. The Taj Mahal in Agra (1653) sets a white marble tomb on a raised plinth in a quartered garden.
