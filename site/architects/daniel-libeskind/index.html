Architect

Daniel Libeskind

Born 1946 · Polish-American · 13 buildings on the wall

Daniel Libeskind (born 1946) is a Polish-American architect, artist and teacher, born in Łódź, whose angular, sharply cut buildings are usually placed within deconstructivism. He founded Studio Daniel Libeskind with his wife, Nina, in 1989, and in 2003 he won the competition to draw the master plan for rebuilding the World Trade Center site in New York.

Read moreShow less

He is best known for the Jewish Museum Berlin (2001), shaped by the history of Jewish life in the city. A line of empty "voids" about 20 metres tall slices through the building, and three axes stand for continuity, emigration and the Holocaust: one leads to the tilted Garden of Exile, another to the Holocaust Tower, an unheated bare concrete silo lit only through a slit in its roof. The Imperial War Museum North in Manchester (2002) is built as three interlocking shards of an imagined globe broken by conflict, standing for air, earth and water. At the Military History Museum in Dresden (2011), a transparent arrowhead cuts through a former arsenal and points towards where the fire-bombing of the city began. Daniel Libeskind's projects are studied for how form can carry memory.
