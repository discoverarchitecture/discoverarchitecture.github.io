Parametric architecture

52 buildings

Parametric architecture designs buildings with algorithms: the architect sets input parameters, and the model changes as they change. Patrik Schumacher coined the term parametricism in 2008 and described it as a successor to modernism and postmodernism. In it, every element of a design can vary and adapt to the others. It rejects both serial repetition and a heap of unrelated pieces, aiming for complex space that stays legible. Its early practitioners included Greg Lynn, Jesse Reiser, Lars Spuybroek and Kas Oosterhuis. Grasshopper, built on Rhino, is a visual programming tool for such models.

Frank Gehry's Guggenheim Bilbao (1997) came before the name, its titanium curves drawn with CATIA, aerospace software. UNStudio's Mercedes-Benz Museum in Stuttgart (2006) winds double-helix ramps on a trefoil plan. Zaha Hadid's MAXXI in Rome (2010) braids concrete galleries under a roof of light fins, and her Heydar Aliyev Center in Baku (2012) folds one continuous surface from plaza to roof, with no line between wall and ground. Shenzhen Bao'an International Airport Terminal 3 (2013), by Studio Fuksas, wraps a 1.6 km terminal in a double honeycomb skin pierced by hexagonal openings.

Questions students ask

What is parametric architecture?

Parametric architecture shapes buildings through algorithms: the designer defines input parameters, and the model updates whenever they shift. Under parametricism, each part of a design is variable and responds to the rest. It avoids both rows of identical repeats and piles of unrelated pieces, seeking spatial complexity that remains readable. Patrik Schumacher introduced the word in 2008, presenting it as following on from modernism and postmodernism.

How does parametric design work?

The architect writes rules rather than drawing each part: an algorithm takes input parameters, and the model updates whenever one changes, so every element can adapt to the others. Grasshopper, a visual programming tool built on Rhino, is one tool for such models. The Canton Tower in Guangzhou (2010) shows the idea: a lattice of inclined steel columns twisted between two rotated ellipses.

Who are the main parametric architects?

Patrik Schumacher, who gave parametricism its name, worked with Zaha Hadid; her office designed many of the most familiar examples, such as MAXXI in Rome (2010) and the Heydar Aliyev Center (2012) in Baku. Lars Spuybroek, Greg Lynn, Kas Oosterhuis and Jesse Reiser were among its early practitioners. The wall also includes work by UNStudio, J. Mayer H. and Studio Fuksas.

What are good examples of parametric architecture?

Four strong examples: Zaha Hadid's Heydar Aliyev Center (2012) in Baku, where one unbroken surface runs from the plaza up over the roof; the Mercedes-Benz Museum (2006) in Stuttgart by UNStudio, whose double-helix ramps sit on a trefoil plan; the Metropol Parasol (2011) in Seville by J. Mayer H., huge timber parasols above a market; and Terminal 3 (2013) of Shenzhen Bao'an International Airport by Studio Fuksas, clad in a double honeycomb skin.
