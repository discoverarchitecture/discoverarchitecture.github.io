Behnisch & Partner

Founded 1966 · German · 4 buildings on the wall

Behnisch & Partner was the Stuttgart architecture practice of Günter Behnisch (1922–2010), who opened his office in 1952 and renamed it Behnisch & Partner in 1966. Born near Dresden, Behnisch served in the German navy in the Second World War, becoming one of its youngest U-boat commanders, and after the war trained as a bricklayer before studying architecture in Stuttgart from 1947. He became one of the best-known German architects associated with deconstructivism. His son Stefan Behnisch set up a separate firm, Behnisch Architekten, in 1989.

Read moreShow less

In 1967 the practice was chosen to plan the park for the 1972 Summer Olympics in Munich. For the Munich Olympic Stadium (1972), Behnisch worked with the engineer Frei Otto on a light tent roof of acrylic glass held by steel cables, one of the first of its kind at this scale and a symbol of the "cheerful Games". The Plenary Building of the Bundestag, Bonn (1992), won in a 1973 competition but begun only in 1987, became the new home of West Germany's parliament. The Norddeutsche Landesbank (NORD/LB) Headquarters in Hanover (2002) came at the end of Behnisch's career.
