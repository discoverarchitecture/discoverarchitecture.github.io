Key featuresA concrete tower in a lattice shell evoking a lotus plant, topped by an observation deck.

About the building

The Egyptian architect Naoum Shebib designed this tower, built between 1956 and 1961, taking ancient Egyptian architecture as his inspiration. Its lattice, partly open, is meant to call up the lotus plant of the pharaohs. At 187 metres, nothing in Egypt stood taller for 37 years, until the overhead power line crossing of the Suez Canal passed it in 1998. A round viewing deck and a revolving restaurant at the top look out across greater Cairo. The tower rises on Gezira Island in the Nile, and some rank it second only to the Pyramids of Giza among the country's landmarks. Documents made public by Major General Adel Shaheen say the money came from the US government through the CIA, as a gift to Gamal Abdel Nasser. The Arab Contractors Company restored it from 2006 to 2009 for £E15 million.

About Cairo Tower

The Cairo Tower (Arabic: برج القاهرة, Borg El-Qāhira) is a free-standing concrete tower in Cairo, Egypt. At 187 m (614 ft), it was the tallest structure in Egypt for 37 years until 1998, when it was surpassed by the Suez Canal overhead powerline crossing. It was the tallest structure in North Africa for 21 years until 1982, when it was surpassed by the Nador transmitter in Morocco. It was the tallest structure in Africa for one year until 1962, when it was surpassed by Sentech Tower in South Africa.

One of Cairo's well-known modern monuments, sometimes considered Egypt's second most famous landmark after the Pyramids of Giza, it stands in the Gezira district on Gezira Island in the River Nile, close to downtown Cairo.
