References. Images by other people that you keep for study: saved from a link, or imported from Cosmos or Pinterest. Roark takes no licence on them beyond showing them that way, and removes them when their author asks.

8. Removing your images

You can delete any image at any time from your Uploads, or from the image's page, or by writing to [email protected]. Deleting an image takes it off Roark and removes its file.

The licence ends for each image when you delete it, except for what was already made from it: copies in backups until they are overwritten, and data derived from it, such as the numbers search uses to compare images. Those cannot show or rebuild your image, and Roark keeps them.

Taking an image out of a collection does not delete it. It stays in your Uploads until you delete it there.

9. Review and takedowns

Every image a member adds is reviewed before others see it, and Roark may decline to show or keep any image. Rights holders can have an image taken down as described on the content and rights page.

10. The data Roark collects

Roark collects what it needs to run accounts, keep the archive safe and learn how it is used. It sells no data, shows no advertising and shares nothing with advertisers.

Your account. Your name, email address, handle and password, stored only as a salted hash. What you choose to add: whether you are a student, an architect or neither; your school and graduation year, or your practice, its city and country; your city, website, links, headline and biography; whether you want to publish your own work. Your profile shows what you put on it, never your email address.

What you do on Roark. Your collections and what you save in them, and the buildings and images you open, which together tune your feed. The page you first arrived on and the site that sent you there, recorded when you sign up.

Images you add. The image, its source and author when known, the building, architect and place you link it to, and the results of its review.

Technical data. Your IP address, kept only as a one-way hash, to apply the limits on sign-ups and uploads per network. Server logs, kept briefly, to keep Roark running and secure.

Analytics. Roark uses PostHog to measure how pages are used: pages opened, searches, saves, and errors, with the browser, device and approximate country they come from. Once you have an account, these are linked to it.

Cookies and storage. One cookie keeps you logged in. Your browser also keeps a few settings: your theme, the collection you last saved to, and, before you sign up, the boards you kept as a guest. PostHog sets its own cookie to recognise a returning browser. Roark sets no advertising cookies.

Who else handles it. Hetzner hosts Roark and its database in Germany. PostHog processes the analytics in the United States. If you pay for anything, Stripe handles the payment and the card; Roark never sees card numbers. OpenAI's moderation service checks images members add for sexual, violent and hateful content. Archive photographs load from Wikimedia Commons and the map from OpenFreeMap, which see your IP address as any website you load does. Each handles the data only to provide its service.

How long. Account data, collections and images are kept while your account exists, and deleted within 30 days of its closing, except for backups until they are overwritten and what the law requires Roark to keep, such as payment records.

Your rights. You can see, correct and download your data, object to its use, and have your account and everything in it deleted. Most of it you can change yourself on your profile; for the rest, and to close your account, write to [email protected]. You may also complain to your data protection authority.
