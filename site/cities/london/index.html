Architecture in London

109 buildings

London's architecture is worth studying because the city has been rebuilt again and again on its old ground. The Great Fire of 1666, which destroyed about nine tenths of the city, was a turning point. Wren's grid plan was set aside: the city was rebuilt on its medieval streets, under a 1667 act asking for brick houses of uniform height and plot size. Pevsner praised Wren's dome for St Paul's as among the most perfect in the world. Most of the city walls came down in the 18th century so London could grow; Victorian London built the world's first underground railway. John Nash laid out Regent Street with James Burton and remodelled Buckingham House into Buckingham Palace.

The Palace of Westminster (1876), by Charles Barry and Augustus Pugin, wraps the parliament in Perpendicular Gothic Revival detail. Battersea Power Station (1955) is one of the world's largest brick buildings. The Barbican Estate (1982) lifts housing, arts and a lake onto walkways above the street. The Lloyd's Building (1986) sets its lifts, stairs and ducts in plug-in towers around a 60-metre atrium. The Shard (2012) became London's first supertall skyscraper.
