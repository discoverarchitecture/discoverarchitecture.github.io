Architect

Norman Foster

Born 1935 · British · 43 buildings on the wall

Norman Foster (born 1935) is an English architect and a pioneer of high-tech architecture, known for steel-and-glass buildings whose structure shapes the design. He founded Foster Associates in 1967, now Foster + Partners, and won the Pritzker Prize in 1999.

Read moreShow less

His early buildings, the Willis Building in Ipswich (1975) and the Sainsbury Centre for Visual Arts in Norwich (1978), tried out open-plan floors on a clear structural grid behind glass walls. The HSBC Main Building in Hong Kong hangs its floors from steel masts and trusses, built from large modules made in Britain and shipped out. The Commerzbank Tower in Frankfurt (1997) is often called the first ecological skyscraper, with sky gardens that bring daylight and fresh air into the offices. In Berlin, Foster rebuilt the Reichstag (1999) for the German parliament and topped it with a walk-in glass dome. With the engineer Michel Virlogeux he designed the Millau Viaduct (2004), and in London he built 30 St Mary Axe (2004). Foster's projects are studied for how structure, daylight and energy use can drive the form of a building.
