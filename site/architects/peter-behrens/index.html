Architect

Peter Behrens

1868–1940 · German · 5 buildings on the wall

Peter Behrens (1868–1940) was a German architect and graphic and industrial designer, and a pioneer of corporate design. Born in Hamburg and trained as a painter, he showed himself an architect with his own Jugendstil house at the Darmstadt Artists' Colony (1901), designed down to the towels. In 1907 he was a founding member of the German Werkbund and began working for the electrical company AEG, designing its logotype, products, publicity and buildings; for this he is considered the first industrial designer in history. Mies van der Rohe, Le Corbusier and Walter Gropius all worked in his office early in their careers.

The Hagen Crematorium (1907), built from 1905 in Hagen-Delstern, was among the early works in which he turned to simple, rectilinear volumes and classical sources. The AEG Turbine Factory in Berlin (1909), his best-known work, is a steel-framed hall about 100 metres long with 15-metre windows set between bare steel columns whose bolts and joints show; it is considered the first attempt to bring restrained modern design to industrial building. The German Embassy, Saint Petersburg (1913) was admired by Hitler, according to Albert Speer.
