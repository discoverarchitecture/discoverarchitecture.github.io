Museum architecture

735 buildings

Museum architecture has to keep a collection safe and open it to the public, and every museum building strikes its own balance between the two. The word museum goes back to a Greek place dedicated to the Muses. Museums began as private collections; in France, the Louvre in Paris became the first public museum. Some museums move into old buildings that have outlived their use; others marry a new wing to an old one. Cities have also used museums as tools for economic growth.

The Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum in New York (1959), called a landmark of 20th-century architecture, replaces floors and rooms with one continuous spiral ramp around a skylit atrium. I. M. Pei's Louvre Pyramid in Paris (1989) lights an underground hall that links the museum's wings. Tate Modern in London (2000) turned a power station into a museum, its Turbine Hall an indoor street. Frank Gehry's Guggenheim Bilbao (1997) shaped its titanium curves with CATIA, aerospace software, and its pull on the city became known as the Bilbao effect. Study them for how visitors move, where light comes from, and what a museum does for its city.

Questions students ask

What makes good museum architecture?

Good museum architecture keeps a collection safe while opening it to the public, and each building balances those two duties in its own way. Look at how visitors move through it, where its light comes from and what it does for its city. In Fort Worth, Louis Kahn's Kimbell Art Museum (1972) brings light into its galleries through a slot in cycloid vaults; New York's Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum (1959) is a single spiral ramp.

Which museums should architecture students study?

Begin with New York's Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum (1959), described as a 20th-century architectural landmark. Then set beside it the Neue Nationalgalerie in Berlin (1968) by Mies van der Rohe, a single glass hall without columns; the Kimbell Art Museum (1972); Carlo Scarpa's Castelvecchio Museum in Verona (1975); Gehry's Guggenheim Bilbao (1997); Libeskind's Jewish Museum Berlin (2001); and SANAA's 21st Century Museum of Contemporary Art, Kanazawa (2004).

What is the Bilbao effect?

The Bilbao effect is the name given to the pull that Frank Gehry's Guggenheim Bilbao, opened in 1997, had on its city. Cities have long treated museums as engines of economic growth, and this museum's titanium curves were designed with CATIA, an aerospace program. For students, it is a case study of what a museum does for its city, not only for its collection.

What are good examples of museums in old buildings?

Good examples include Tate Modern in London (2000), a former power station whose Turbine Hall now works as an indoor street, and the Castelvecchio Museum in Verona (1975), Carlo Scarpa's reworking of a medieval castle. Some museums take over buildings whose first use has ended; others attach new wings to old buildings, as the Louvre Pyramid (1989) by I. M. Pei in Paris does, lighting an underground hall that connects the old museum's wings.
