Architecture in Los Angeles

33 buildings

Los Angeles architecture is studied above all for its modern houses: the Case Study Houses changed how homes were designed. Arts & Architecture launched the programme in 1945 and asked for houses "capable of duplication", not one-off performances. Rudolph Schindler's house of 1922 was a cooperative live/work space for two young families and set a precedent for California architecture. Frank Lloyd Wright called Hollyhock House his "California Romanza". Googie, the roadside Space Age style, was named after a Hollywood coffee shop. In 1978 the Los Angeles Conservancy was founded to save the Central Library from demolition.

The Schindler House (1922) is built of tilt-up concrete slabs with glass slits, its L-shaped studios each opening onto a garden court. Charles and Ray Eames's Eames House (1949) is a Case Study House assembled from off-the-shelf industrial parts. Pierre Koenig's Stahl House (Case Study House #22), from 1960, cantilevers an L-shaped steel and glass pavilion over the Hollywood Hills; its photograph is one of the most famous of Los Angeles. Frank Gehry's Walt Disney Concert Hall (2003) wraps a vineyard-style hall in curved stainless steel, a cheaper skin than the stone first planned.
