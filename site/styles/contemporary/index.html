Contemporary architecture

1035 buildings

Contemporary architecture is the architecture of the 21st century, from 2000 to today, and no single style leads it. It is shaped by method more than style: new structural systems make buildings taller, lighter and stronger than in the 20th century, and designs are modelled in 3D on computers. Some designers favour natural, ecological materials such as stone, wood and lime; energy efficiency over a building's whole life is a primary goal of sustainable design. The field is global, with important new buildings in China, Russia and India.

The Burj Khalifa in Dubai (2010), once the tallest building in the world, rises on a Y-shaped buttressed core that steps back in a spiral against the wind. Shigeru Ban's Centre Pompidou-Metz (2010) covers three stacked gallery tubes with a woven grid of glued laminated timber. Snøhetta's Oslo Opera House (2008) slopes a marble roof into the fjord, open for anyone to walk on. OMA's CCTV Headquarters in Beijing (2012) joins two leaning towers with a 75-metre cantilever. Herzog & de Meuron's Elbphilharmonie in Hamburg (2017) sets a glass crown on a brick warehouse, with a public plaza between old and new.
